# Session 3 deck brief — bring the facilitator deck in line with the workbook

This file is written to be pasted into Claude as a prompt. Part A is the instruction. Part B is the exact content of Session 3 as built in the Digital Leader workbook (the app participants use in the room). Part C is the slide-by-slide list of what the current deck says that the app no longer says. Everything in quotation marks in Parts B and C is verbatim from the app.

## Part A — the instruction to paste

> You are updating the facilitator deck for **Session 3 of the EDMECA AI Enablement Programme** (Property Point | Growthpoint Enterprise Development, Cohort Seven). The deck is currently titled *"Business Development and Market Access — Winning the Work"* and is framed around tenders and proposals. The participant workbook for this session has since been built **quotation-first**: the organising frame is the enquiry-to-quote flow, and a formal RFQ or tender is one buyer type among four, not the frame. The deck must now match the workbook, because participants work in the workbook while the deck is on screen and the two cannot use different words for the same thing.
>
> **Why the shift:** the cohort's own Session 2 data. Of the seven real participants who completed Session 2, five chose a quote-shaped process to redesign (*enquiry to quote*, *quote to cash*, *Quotation*, *Request for Quotation to purchase order*, *From Enquiry to Invoice*). None chose a tender or a proposal. The room's live opportunity is a quote request. The deck already concedes the frameworks are buyer-agnostic — *"the public tender and the private proposal are the same document wearing a different suit"* — so the structure survives; the vocabulary and two artefacts change.
>
> **What to do:**
> 1. Read Part B (the workbook as built) and Part C (the deltas) below.
> 2. Rewrite the deck slide by slide so that every exercise instruction, list, label, quadrant, table row and prompt on a slide matches the workbook **word for word** where the workbook has a version of it. Where the deck has teaching content the workbook does not replicate (the ten laws, the buyer's four questions, the three assets, R/D/C/F, the sector cards, the four measures), keep it, but swap tender/bid/proposal vocabulary for quote vocabulary where it appears, unless the slide is specifically about the RFQ/tender buyer type.
> 3. Keep the deck's voice exactly: short declarative sentences, South African business English, no adjectives, no mission-statement language, footers that land a single line. Keep the facilitator-note structure on every slide (CLOCK / LAND / SAY / ASK / DO / WATCH / NEXT, plus FALLBACK on demo slides) and keep the existing timings — the four blocks are 30 / 40 / 75 / 35 minutes with a 10-minute break between blocks 2 and 3, Exercise 1 is 20 minutes, Exercise 2 is 37 minutes.
> 4. Produce two things: (a) the revised slide text and facilitator notes for every slide that changes, and (b) a change log — one line per slide — saying what changed and which workbook part it now matches.
> 5. Do not invent new frameworks. If something in the workbook has no home in the deck, say so in the change log and propose where it goes; do not silently add slides.
> 6. Leave slide 8 (the *X.XX OUT OF 4* PRE-assessment placeholder) exactly as it is, including its BLOCKED note. Leave the two learning outcomes not covered by the workbook (buying unit and journey; one-sentence value proposition) out of scope — they are being handled separately.
> 7. Flag, do not resolve, the six-block naming question in C.14.

## Part B — Session 3 as built in the workbook

**Header:** *AI Enablement Programme · Session 3 of 6* · session tab name **Market Access**.

**Five parts** (the in-app stepper, in order): Part 1 Choose Your Ground · Part 2 Your Evidence · Part 3 Capability Statement · Part 4 Quotation Template · Part 5 Follow-up & Pilot.

### Part 1 — Choose Your Ground (Exercise 1 · 20 minutes)

Lead: *"Two outputs in twenty minutes. Your go-to-market outline first, then two live quote requests scored and ranked."*

**Card 1 · "Go to market, in three questions"** — hint: *"Targeting, positioning and channels. Answer these three and most of your quoting decisions make themselves."*

| Field | Hint under the field | Example shown in the field |
|---|---|---|
| Who you serve | "The client type you are built to deliver for, not everyone with a budget." | Facilities managers at listed property funds, Gauteng |
| What you are known for | "The one thing you do better, said in words a client would use — without the words 'quality' or 'professional'." | After-hours response inside two hours, every time |
| Where they find you | "The channels where that client actually looks for someone like you." | Referrals, framework panels, LinkedIn, walk-in enquiries |

Two live validations: if "everyone" appears in *Who you serve*, the app says *"'Everyone' is not a market — it is an absence of a decision. Name the client type you are actually built to serve."* If "quality" or "professional" appears in *What you are known for*, it says *"'quality' says nothing a competitor would not also claim. Say what you actually do."*

**Card 2 · "What is live right now"** — hint: *"Every real enquiry, quote request or RFQ in front of you, named. Aim for four or five, then score two of them out of five on each question: Can we win it? Is it worth winning? Can we deliver it?"* Button: **Add a quote request**.

Each quote request has: a name (example: *Growthpoint — quarterly HVAC service, three sites*); **Who is asking?** (one of four buyer types); **They want it by** (a date); three scores; and a button *"Take this one through this afternoon"*.

The four buyer types, verbatim (these replace the deck's four breakout corners):

| Buyer type | Description |
|---|---|
| New client enquiry | "Someone who found you, and is comparing you with two others." |
| Existing client, repeat work | "A client you already serve, asking for the next job." |
| Main contractor or panel | "Pricing a package for a main contractor, or a call-off from a framework." |
| Formal RFQ or tender | "A written request with a closing date, returnables and a scoring sheet." |

The three scores are **Winnability /5**, **Value /5**, **Deliverability /5**, shown with a running total **out of 15**.

**Card 3 · "Where should you quote first?"** — a 2×2 grid, **Value** up the side (low at the bottom), **Winnability** across (low at the left). Scored quote requests plot onto it live. Quadrant labels, verbatim:

- Top-left: *Long shots · build the relationship*
- Top-right: *Quote here first*
- Bottom-left: *Leave them*
- Bottom-right: *Quick quotes · low effort*

Hint: *"Value up the side, winnability across. Top right is where you quote: worth winning and winnable. Deliverability decides which one in that box you start with."* Warning box: *"A warning worth taking: do not open with the biggest, most contested request on the board. The odds are poor, and one lost quote kills the appetite for the next five. Win a smaller one first."*

**Card 4 · "Five kinds of wasted quote"** — hint: *"Every quote you lost that you should never have written is one of these five, or a combination of them. Count last year's losses against them."*

| Kind | Line |
|---|---|
| Wrong client | "They were never going to buy from a firm your size" |
| Wrong scale | "Too big to deliver, or too small to be worth the paperwork" |
| Wrong margin | "You can win it, and it will cost you money to do it" |
| Wrong timing | "Priced against a budget that was fixed before you were asked" |
| No relationship | "Your first contact was the request itself" |

Output line: *"Output: a go-to-market outline, and two quote requests scored and ranked."*

### Part 2 — You already have the evidence (Exercise 2 · Step 1)

Lead: *"Ten to fourteen jobs, with a client, a value and a date. Nothing invented — AI cannot invent your track record, and you must not let it try. It can only organise what you give it."*

**Card 1 · "Your track record"** — hint: *"Work down the evidence ladder in order: the jobs (client, scope, value, date, outcome), the people, the proof, the numbers, the words. Every rung is something you already own and have probably never written down. This is what goes out behind every quote."*

Table columns: **Client | Scope | Value | Date | Outcome**. Counter: *"N captured · aim for 10 to 14"*. Empty state: *"No jobs captured yet. Start with your invoice list — the last three years."*

**Card 2 · "What stays yours, what AI drafts"** — hint: *"Go through a quote you send section by section and put every part into one of these three columns. Keep the price and the promises. Give away the typing."* Twelve items, each sorted into one of three columns; the app's answer key is the column shown here. Putting price anywhere but *Stays yours* turns the button red; any other mismatch shows *"Have another look at this one."*

| Stays yours | AI assists | Mechanical |
|---|---|---|
| Price, rates and commercial terms | Scope description and how you will do the work | Document naming and filing |
| Any claim about what you have done | Capability statement and CVs | Follow-up reminders and validity dates |
| Delivery dates you commit to | Reformatting into the client's RFQ or order format | Pipeline stage updates |
| The client conversation itself | Checking the quote answers everything they asked for | Standard quote covering notes |

Footer: *"A claim you cannot evidence is a claim you delete. A number you did not work out yourself is a number you do not send."*

### Part 3 — Your capability statement (Exercise 2 · Step 2)

Lead: *"Two pages a client can read in four minutes. It goes out with every quote, so refine it until a stranger would shortlist you."*

**Card 1 · "Build the prompt"** — hint: *"The prompt runs on the N jobs you captured in Part 2. Name the sector you are writing for, build the prompt, run it in your AI tool, then paste the result back in."* One field: *Writing for a client in which sector?* (example: commercial property, facilities management). Buttons: **Build prompt**, **Copy prompt**. Then a box labelled *Your capability statement* to paste the result back into.

The prompt the app builds, verbatim (curly braces are filled from the participant's own entries):

```
Turn these {N} projects and this company background into a two-page capability statement for a {sector} client. Use their language, not mine. Lead with what we have delivered, not with how long we have existed. Flag anything you had to assume.

COMPANY: {business name}
WHO WE SERVE: {who you serve}
WHAT WE ARE KNOWN FOR: {what you are known for}

OUR PROJECTS:
1. {client} — {scope} — {value} — {date} — {outcome}
2. …

Include: one line saying exactly what we do; capability areas in a client's language; the project list with values and dates; the team with tickets and years; and a differentiator stated in one sentence. Do not invent a project, a value or a date I have not given you.
```

**Card 2 · "Would a stranger shortlist you?"** — hint: *"The test is simple: could a client who has never met you decide to shortlist you from this page alone? Tick what is genuinely true of your draft."* Five tick-boxes:

1. It leads with delivery, not with history
2. Every project carries a value and a client type
3. The numbers are checkable, not adjectives
4. The team is named, with real tickets
5. It fits on two pages and reads in four minutes

Footer: *"What should not be on the page: adjectives, mission statements, and stock photographs."*

### Part 4 — Your quotation template & completeness check (Exercise 2 · Steps 3 and 4)

Lead: *"Working on {the chosen quote}. Keep the structure, change the substance. The price is yours — you are automating the words around the number, never the number."*

**Card 1 · "Point the recipe at a quote"** — hint: *"The prompt recipe from Session 1, now pointed at the words around your quote — the covering note, the scope, the assumptions and the exclusions. The context block is the one that matters most today, because that is where your evidence goes."* Then: *"Role, Task, Context and Example are filled in from Parts 1 to 3 — your enquiry, your positioning, your evidence and your capability statement. Edit any of them. Requirements and Format are the two only you can supply: they come off the enquiry in front of you and your own terms, so nothing is guessed for you here."*

The six blocks as labelled in the app (label — what goes in it — example text in the field):

| Block | What goes in it | Example in the field |
|---|---|---|
| Role | An estimator and quote writer in your sector | You are an experienced estimator for a mechanical services contractor |
| Task | What the quote must contain | Draft the covering note, scope of work, assumptions and exclusions |
| Context | The enquiry and your evidence | Uses the enquiry you picked and your evidence table |
| Requirements | What the client asked for, and your terms | Validity 30 days. Payment terms. Lead time. Anything they specified |
| Format | Your quote layout, or theirs | Covering note, scope table, assumptions, exclusions, validity. Price table left blank for me |
| Example | One quote you were happy with | e.g. Match the tone and layout of the quote below, not its prices |

Four blocks arrive pre-filled from earlier parts and are marked *· carried forward*:

- **Role** → *"You are an experienced estimator and quote writer for {business}, working with {sector} clients."*
- **Task** depends on the chosen quote's buyer type:
  - New client enquiry → *"Draft the covering note, scope of work, and assumptions and exclusions for a first quote to a new client"*
  - Existing client, repeat work → *"Draft a short covering note and scope for a repeat job, referencing the work we have already done for them"*
  - Main contractor or panel → *"Draft the scope, inclusions and exclusions for a package price to a main contractor"*
  - Formal RFQ or tender → *"Draft the response sections the RFQ asks for — scope, how we will do the work, and a checklist of everything they require"*
- **Context** → *"The enquiry is {name} — a {buyer type}, wanted by {date}. We serve {who you serve}. We are known for {what you are known for}. Use the {N} jobs in our evidence list as the only source for anything we claim about ourselves."*
- **Example** → *"Match the tone and structure of the past quote included below, not its prices."* (or, if no past quote was attached, *"Match the tone of our capability statement, included below."*)

Under the Example block is a drop zone: *"Drop a quote you sent and were happy with — PDF, Word (.docx) or text — or choose a file. It is read on your own laptop and only the text is kept. Nothing is uploaded."* The text of that past quote (capped at 6,000 characters) goes into the prompt.

Buttons: **Build prompt**, **Copy prompt**, **Rebuild from Parts 1-3**. Then a box labelled *Your quotation template* to paste the result back into, with the placeholder *"Paste what your AI tool produced. Every price should still read [PRICE] — you fill those in. Keep the structure: it is what you reuse on the next quote…"*

The prompt the app builds, verbatim in shape (only filled blocks appear):

```
ROLE: {role}

TASK: {task}

CONTEXT: {context}

REQUIREMENTS: {requirements}

FORMAT: {format}

EXAMPLE: {example}

THE ENQUIRY: {the chosen quote's name}

MY EVIDENCE — use only these, and do not invent any others:
1. {client} — {scope} — {value} — {date} — {outcome}
…

OUR CAPABILITY STATEMENT — for tone, and for the facts about us:
{the capability statement from Part 3}

A PAST QUOTE I WAS HAPPY WITH — match its tone and structure, not its prices:
{the text of the past quote}

Leave every price, rate and total as [PRICE] for me to fill in. Do not estimate a number. List every assumption you made, and mark anything you could not answer from what I gave you as TO CONFIRM.
```

**Card 2 · "Check it against the enquiry"** — hint: *"Read it back against what the client actually asked for. List what they asked for that this quote does not yet answer, and name who supplies it. A quote that misses one line item loses to one that didn't, whatever the price."* Two columns per row: *What they asked for that isn't answered yet — e.g. an after-hours rate* | *Who supplies it — one name*. Button: **Add a gap**.

### Part 5 — Follow up, measure it, set the date

Lead: *"More work is lost to silence after the quote goes out than to a weak quote. This part is the silence, fixed."*

**Card 1 · "Your follow-up pack"** — hint: *"Five messages you send every week, written once in your voice and reused for years. The prompt is built from the quote you picked; run it, then paste the five back in and edit until they sound like you."* The five, verbatim:

1. A follow-up that gives them a reason to reply
2. A quote covering note, in your voice
3. An escalation that stays professional
4. A holding reply for when you need more time
5. A thank-you that asks for the next job

Buttons: **Build prompt**, **Copy prompt**. Paste-back box labelled *Your five messages*. The prompt the app builds, verbatim:

```
Write five short messages I can reuse for every quote I send. Plain, direct, in my voice — no sales language, nothing that sounds desperate.

WHO WE ARE: {business}, known for {what you are known for}.
WHO WE SERVE: {who you serve}
THE CURRENT QUOTE: {the chosen quote's name}, wanted by {date}. Use "Client A" for the client's name; I will put the real name back in.

THE FIVE:
1. A follow-up that gives them a reason to reply
2. A quote covering note, in your voice
3. An escalation that stays professional
4. A holding reply for when you need more time
5. A thank-you that asks for the next job

Each one under 120 words, ready to paste into email or WhatsApp. Give each one a reason for the client to reply, not just a nudge. Then give me the follow-up and the escalation again, once firmer and once warmer, so I can pick the register.
```

**Card 2 · "Your pipeline in five stages"** — the table, verbatim, followed by *"Put your quotes on the board"*, where each quote request from Part 1 is assigned a stage:

| Stage | What it means | What moves it on | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Identified | You have heard about it | Score it on the three questions | This week |
| 2 Qualified | It passed the score | Decide to quote, and name who owns it | 48 hours |
| 3 Quoted | It is with the client | Log the date, set the follow-up | On the day |
| 4 In discussion | They came back to you | Answer fast, and answer in writing | Same day |
| 5 Won or lost | A decision was made | Ask why, either way, and write it down | Always |

**"Pick your pilot"** — eyebrow *THE WHOLE POINT OF TODAY*. Lead: *"One quote. Six fields. Without an owner and two dates this stays a good intention instead of becoming a quote that went out."* Six fields, verbatim labels (with the example text shown in the field):

1. The quote — which one, named exactly *(pre-filled with the quote chosen in Part 1)*
2. The asset — the statement or template you use *(e.g. Capability statement + quotation template)*
3. The action — what you send, in one sentence *(e.g. Send the quote with the covering note and the capability statement attached)*
4. The owner — one name, not "the team"
5. The date — when the quote goes out, this week
6. The follow-up — the day you chase it, in your diary

The app refuses to save the pilot without the quote, the owner and the send date.

**"Your business development rhythm"** — eyebrow *THE ONGOING TOOL*. Lead: *"A one-page rhythm you can run in about fifteen minutes a week."* Tick-boxes:

- DAILY: *Reply to every enquiry the same day* · *Log every new enquiry you hear about*
- WEEKLY: *Move every quote one stage, or kill it* · *Send the follow-ups that are due*
- MONTHLY: *Add the month's new work to your statement* · *Review the win rate and where the losses came from*

**"Before Session 4: keep the momentum"** — verbatim: *"Send the pilot on the date you wrote, to the client you named. Fill the pipeline — every live quote on the page and scored, five minutes on Friday. Finish the pack — the five messages and a second quotation template, from the same evidence. Next session is Costing, Pricing and Financial Intelligence. Bring the pricing on the quote you built today — we find out whether it actually makes money."*

Closing button: **Save my pilot** → *"Pilot saved. Now send the quote."*
